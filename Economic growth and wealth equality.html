Economic growth and wealth equality

Back to interactive games · Stagflation

Faster economic growth and a more equal distribution of wealth can conflict.
A policy that shifts LRAS right can raise productive capacity, but the gains can go to owners of capital.
A policy that redistributes income can lower the Gini coefficient, but higher costs or weaker incentives can slow growth.
Education and training can support both objectives. Press for the scenario.

Scenario

Economic scenario

The economy is at full employment. Aggregate demand and short-run aggregate supply cross on long-run aggregate supply. Real GDP growth is 1.0%. Real output and full-employment output are both £2,800 billion, so there is no output gap. Unemployment is 4.5%. Skills are uneven, and poorer regions have not shared in income and wealth.

Wealth is unequally distributed. The wealth Gini coefficient is 0.42. The Lorenz curve bows away from the line of equality. Owners of capital and land hold a large share of assets. Low-paid workers and poorer regions have seen weak gains.

The government has a large budget deficit and only has £15 billion to spend.

Your task

Bring the economy to all four of these results:

  • Economic growth at or above 2.5%.
  • A wealth Gini coefficient at or below 0.35.
  • Unemployment kept at or below 5.5%.
  • Productive capacity at least £60 billion higher, so the growth is not only a short-run rise in demand.

Read the diagram and the Lorenz curve first. The economy starts on LRAS, and the wealth Gini coefficient is 0.42. The government can spend only £15 billion. Then choose policies. Supply-side policy can shift LRAS right. Demand-side policy shifts AD right and raises growth only in the short run, which opens a positive output gap. Progressive tax, welfare benefits and a higher minimum wage pull the Lorenz curve inward, but they can shift SRAS or LRAS left. Education and training is one of the few policies that supports both objectives. Regional development can also narrow inequality and raise capacity.

  • Economic growth at or above 2.5%.
  • A wealth Gini coefficient at or below 0.35.
  • Unemployment at or below 5.5%.
  • Productive capacity at least £60 billion higher.

Policy choices

Press a policy button to use it. Press the same button again to turn it off. The diagram does not move until you press Shift AD, Shift SRAS or Shift LRAS. Reset policies returns the economy to full employment, with the wealth Gini coefficient at 0.42.

Policies to increase economic growth. These aim to increase real GDP and shift LRAS to the right.

Education and training Choose £10 billion or £15 billion. Aggregate demand and short-run aggregate supply each shift by that amount, so real GDP rises by the same amount.
Infrastructure investment Choose £10 billion or £15 billion. Aggregate demand and short-run aggregate supply each shift by that amount, so real GDP rises by the same amount.
Business investment incentives Investment allowances, corporation tax reductions and research and development grants.
Promoting competition Deregulation, reducing barriers to entry and preventing monopolistic behaviour.
Labour market reforms Easier hiring and firing, weaker trade-union power and better occupational mobility. Lowers wage costs and structural unemployment. Can widen inequality.
Government investment in R&D Choose £5 billion or £10 billion. Aggregate demand and short-run aggregate supply each shift by that amount, so real GDP rises by the same amount.
Demand-side policies Lower interest rates, AD shifts right in the short run.

Policies to increase wealth equality. These aim to reduce inequalities in income and wealth.

Education and training is listed above. It is one of the few policies that helps both objectives.

Progressive taxation Higher earners pay a larger proportion of income in tax, through higher bands, capital gains tax or a wealth tax.
Welfare benefits and transfers Universal Credit, state pensions, disability benefits and child benefit.
National minimum wage Raises the earnings of low-paid workers and reduces in-work poverty.
Regional development Enterprise zones, infrastructure in deprived areas and grants for firms locating in poorer regions. Also supports growth.
