XED = %ΔQA ÷ %ΔPB. Cross elasticity of demand (XED) measures how much the quantity demanded of one good changes when the price of another good changes. It tells you whether two goods are substitutes, complements, or unrelated, and how strongly they are connected.

A movement along a demand curve happens only when the good’s own price changes.

Here, the price of good A stays the same — it’s the price of good B that changes.

So consumers aren’t reacting to A becoming cheaper or dearer; they’re reacting to B becoming more expensive. That’s an external factor, which causes a shift of the entire demand curve.
